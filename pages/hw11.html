SDE Simulation with Euler-Maruyama

The goal of this assignment is to simulate a **Wiener Process** (Brownian Motion) using a numerical method for Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs) called the Euler-Maruyama method.

As an optional task, the simulator has been generalized to handle other common SDEs, such as Geometric Brownian Motion (used in finance) and Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (mean-reverting).

1. Stochastic Differential Equations (SDE)

A generic SDE usually takes the form:

\[ dX_t = \mu(X_t, t)dt + \sigma(X_t, t)dW_t \]

Where:

  • \( X_t \): The state of the process at time \( t \).
  • \( \mu(X_t, t) \): The Drift term (deterministic part).
  • \( \sigma(X_t, t) \): The Diffusion term (stochastic part).
  • \( dW_t \): The increment of a Wiener process (Brownian motion).

2. The Euler-Maruyama Method

Just as the Euler method approximates ordinary differential equations, the Euler-Maruyama method approximates SDEs.

We discretize time into steps of size \( \Delta t \). The recursive formula to find the next value \( X_{n+1} \) is:

\[ X_{n+1} = X_n + \mu(X_n, t_n)\Delta t + \sigma(X_n, t_n)\Delta W_n \]

Where \( \Delta W_n \) is a random increment drawn from a Normal distribution: \( \Delta W_n \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Delta t) \).
In code, we simulate this as \( \sqrt{\Delta t} \cdot Z \), where \( Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \).

3. Models Implemented

  • Wiener Process (Standard Brownian Motion):
    \( dX_t = 0 dt + 1 dW_t \)
    (Drift \(\mu=0\), Diffusion \(\sigma=1\)).
  • Geometric Brownian Motion (Black-Scholes):
    \( dX_t = \mu X_t dt + \sigma X_t dW_t \)
    (Used for stock prices. The noise scales with the value \(X\)).
  • Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (Mean Reverting):
    \( dX_t = \theta(\mu - X_t) dt + \sigma dW_t \)
    (The process tends to drift back towards a long-term mean \(\mu\)).
